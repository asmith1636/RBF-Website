What Site Work Does a Commercial Building Need?

Commercial site work includes clearing and demolition, earthwork and grading, underground utilities, storm water management, paving, concrete and final grading — often a large share of total project cost.

By the RBF Construction team · Published October 21, 2026

Short answer: Before a commercial building goes up, the site typically needs clearing or demolition, excavation and grading, underground utilities (water, sanitary and storm), storm water management such as a retention pond, a paved parking lot with curbs, sidewalks and final grading. Site work can be a large share of total project cost, so plan it early.

1. Site Evaluation

Start with a survey, soils information and a review of the local site plan requirements. Poor soils, high groundwater or a lack of nearby utilities can change the budget significantly.

2. Clearing and Demolition

Existing buildings, pavement, trees and foundations have to come out. See demolition.

3. Earthwork and Grading

The site is cut and filled to plan elevations and the building pad is prepared. Balancing cuts and fills on site avoids costly trucking. See excavation and grading.

4. Underground Utilities

Water service, sanitary sewer and storm sewer are installed and connected to municipal systems, along with manholes, catch basins and, for restaurants, grease interceptors. See underground utilities.

5. Storm Water Management

Most Michigan communities require new developments to control storm water runoff, often with retention or detention ponds or underground storage, plus soil erosion and sedimentation control during construction.

6. Paving and Concrete

Parking lots, drive aisles and drive-thru lanes are built on a compacted aggregate base, then finished with curbs, gutters, sidewalks and accessible ramps. See parking lots and concrete.

7. Final Grading and Restoration

Topsoil, final grades and landscaping areas finish the site and direct water away from the building.
