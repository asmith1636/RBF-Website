Planning a Kitchen or Bathroom Remodel in Southeast Michigan

Define scope first, set a budget with a contingency, make selections before demolition, confirm permits, and hire a contractor who manages trades and inspections.

By the RBF Construction team · Updated October 23, 2026

Short answer: Decide your scope, set a realistic budget with a contingency, finalize selections before demolition, confirm permits with your city or township, and hire a contractor who manages every trade and inspection.

1. Define the Scope

The biggest cost driver in any kitchen or bath remodel is whether you’re keeping the layout. Replacing cabinets, counters and fixtures in place costs far less than moving plumbing, removing walls or relocating appliances. Decide early which category you’re in.

2. Set a Budget — and a Contingency

Once walls and floors are opened up, older Michigan homes can reveal hidden issues: outdated wiring, undersized drains, water damage or subfloor problems. Plan a contingency for surprises so they don’t derail the project.

3. Make Selections Before Demolition

Cabinets, specialty tile and some fixtures can take weeks to arrive. Finalizing and ordering selections before demolition is the single best way to shorten the time your kitchen or bathroom is out of service.

4. Understand Permits

Most Michigan municipalities require permits for work that changes plumbing, electrical, mechanical or structural elements. Permits mean inspections, which protect you and your home’s resale value. A good contractor pulls permits and schedules inspections for you.

5. Think About How You’ll Live During the Remodel

For kitchens, set up a temporary kitchen with a microwave, refrigerator and coffee maker. For bathrooms, plan around having another bathroom available. Ask your contractor about dust control and daily cleanup.

6. Plan for the Long Term

If you plan to stay, consider aging-in-place features now: curbless showers, grab-bar blocking, wider doorways, better lighting and easy-to-reach storage. They cost far less to include during a remodel than to add later.

7. Choose the Right Contractor

  • Do they manage all trades, permits and inspections?
  • Will someone supervise on site?
  • Do they provide a written scope and schedule?
  • Can they handle structural changes if needed?
